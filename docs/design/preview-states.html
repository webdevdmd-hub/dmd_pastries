Six states that decide whether a cashier makes a mistake

The UI rebuild plan names these six states and assigns each an owner. None of them has a design. This is that design, drawn at v3 token values so it can be built from rather than interpreted. Ordered worst-consequence first.

C0.6 Irreversible Counter register · 48px

Confirm before something irreversible

Today: zero AlertDialog usages across 584 components. Void, refund, post a journal entry, close a period and delete a product all fire immediately or confirm through ad-hoc markup. This is the one surface that exists solely to prevent a mistake.

void a sale — counter

Void this sale?

This voids sale #1042 for AED 75.60 and posts a reversing journal entry. It cannot be undone.

Reversing entry — 4100 Sales Returns / 1010 Cash
  • State the consequence, with the money in it. "Are you sure?" tells a cashier nothing. The sale number and the amount are what they check against the receipt in their hand, so both are in the body, in Geist Mono with tabular figures.
  • Name the accounting effect. A void is not a delete — it posts a reversing entry. Showing the two accounts means the owner reconciling that evening already knows what happened.
  • The safe action is focused, and sits last. Keep sale carries the --ring focus and the rightmost position, so a stray Enter or a blind tap at the natural thumb rest keeps the sale.
  • The destructive action is outline, never a filled red button. A solid red button reads as the primary action and invites the tap it is meant to slow down. Danger lives in --danger-text on --card, with the tint arriving only on hover.
  • Both buttons are 48px. Equal size, because making the destructive one smaller to "discourage" it just makes it easier to mis-hit the wrong one.
  • No green. Green is money-committing only. Confirming a void is not taking payment.

Acceptance: every destructive action in the app routes through this primitive; the body always contains the identifier and the amount; the destructive control is never autoFocus and never the filled variant. Add an ESLint selector banning onClick handlers named void*/delete*/refund* outside a confirm wrapper.

C0.7 Money Counter register

The charge failed

Today: one of 103 unstyled toasts, four seconds, then gone. The cart looks unchanged and the cashier cannot tell whether the customer was charged. That ambiguity is how a sale gets rung twice.

failed charge — counter
Charge failed

No payment was taken. The card reader did not respond. The cart is unchanged — sale #1043, AED 75.60.

  • Answer the only question that matters first: was money taken? "No payment was taken" is the first line, in --foreground while the rest of the message is muted. A cashier reads one line before looking back at the customer.
  • Money failures do not auto-dismiss. The plan says ≥6s; that is still a timer on a question about money. This one persists until acknowledged or resolved. A four-second toast that vanished is indistinguishable from no toast at all.
  • Confirm the cart survived. The second-worst outcome after a double charge is a cashier re-ringing eight items they still have.
  • Retry is the commit variant. It is the one action here that moves money, so it is the one green thing on the screen.
  • Never the only report of a lost-data failure. If the sale cannot be recovered, it belongs in the till surface, not only in a transient.

Acceptance: audit all 103 toast call sites into three classes — money (persistent, danger, states the money verdict), operational (6s), and confirmation (3s). Any toast reporting a failed mutation on a money path must be persistent; assert it in a test rather than in review.

E5 Blocks work Counter register · persistent

The till is offline

Today: nothing. 25 files reference onLine/offline ad hoc. The plan's own words: "a cashier who does not know the till is offline will keep ringing sales."

offline — counter
Counter 1 · Amina Sale #1044
Offline Sales can't be completed until the connection returns.
2×  Almond croissant21.00
1×  Sourdough loaf18.00
4×  Date maamoul36.60
Total 75.60
Charging is off while the till is offline. The cart is saved.
  • Warning is not enough — stop the sale. A bar a cashier can look past still lets them ring eight sales into nothing. Charge is disabled while offline, with the reason stated directly under it rather than in a tooltip nobody hovers on a touchscreen.
  • Say what it means, not what it is. "Offline" is the system's word. "Sales can't be completed" is the cashier's.
  • Persistent, undismissable, and it survives navigation. It belongs in the counter layout, not in a route. A dismissable offline warning is a warning that gets dismissed at the start of a rush.
  • Promise the cart. "The cart is saved" is the difference between waiting and re-ringing.
  • Dot plus text. Colour never carries this alone.
  • Retry is manual and visible. Silent background retries leave the cashier guessing; one button and a status they can act on beats a spinner.

Acceptance: one provider owns connectivity; the bar renders in the counter layout; every money-committing control subscribes to it and disables with a stated reason. Test: with the network offline, Charge is disabled on /pos and in pos-checkout-dialog.

E5 Silent today Ledger register

Page 1 loaded, page 2 failed

Today: silence, or a spinner that never ends. The reader has no way to know the list is incomplete — which, on a trial balance or a stock list, means a decision made on partial data.

partial collection — ledger
Product SKU On hand Value (AED)
Almond croissantBK-011442441.00
Sourdough loafBK-020817306.00
Date maamoulBK-033196878.40
Pistachio baklavaBK-04278184.00
Showing 50 of 214. Couldn't load the rest — totals below are incomplete.
  • It belongs in the list, at the seam. The failure is about these rows, so it renders where the rows stop. A toast detaches the message from the thing it describes, and toasts leave.
  • Warning, not danger. Something did load. Danger would misrepresent the severity and devalue the colour where it means "no payment was taken".
  • Count both numbers. 50 of 214 tells the reader how wrong the view is. "Some items failed to load" does not.
  • Say the totals are incomplete. This is the part that prevents a wrong decision. A partial list with a confident-looking total is worse than an error, and any totals row must visibly inherit the warning.
  • Retry loads forward, never restarts. The 50 rows already read stay put and keep scroll position.

Acceptance: any paginated or incrementally-loaded collection can render partial; a totals row on a partial collection carries the warning treatment. Audit every route with a totals row against this.

C0.5 + E4 Two languages today Both registers

Loading

Today: 96 files with two ad-hoc treatments — Skeleton in some, animate-pulse in others. On counter hardware over bakery wifi this is the most-seen transient state in the app. Building C0.5 without the E4 sweep adds a third language rather than replacing two.

counter — tile-shaped, matches the real grid
ledger — row-shaped, 44px rows, column widths preserved
AccountCodeDebitCredit
  • The skeleton is the shape of the thing that arrives. Counter gets tiles at the real 146px minimum and 168px height; Ledger gets 44px rows at the real column widths. A generic grey block is why the page jumps when data lands.
  • No spinners in either register. A spinner says "something is happening"; a skeleton says "here is what is coming and where".
  • One tint, --muted stepped down. No shimmer sweep — a moving gradient across a full product grid is the animation a cashier is trying to flick past.
  • Honour prefers-reduced-motion. The pulse becomes a flat tint, not nothing — the placeholder must still read as pending.
  • The sweep is the deliverable, not the primitive. E4 replaces all 96 sites, or this section made things worse.

Acceptance: zero animate-pulse outside the primitive, enforced by an ESLint selector once E4 lands. Loading a collection route causes no layout shift when data arrives.

E1 + E2 Indistinguishable today Ledger register

Empty, filtered, failed

Today: mostly "No items found." for all three. The plan requires five visibly distinct treatments per collection route. The three below are the ones that get confused with each other, because they look identical and differ entirely in what the user should do next.

the three that get confused — ledger

No products yet

Add your first product and it will show up here, ready to sell at the counter.

announces: "No products yet. Add your first product."

No products match "sourd"

214 products exist. Try a shorter search, or clear the category filter.

announces: "No results for sourd. 214 products exist."

Couldn't load products

The request failed. Nothing has changed — your products are still there.

announces: "Couldn't load products. Nothing has changed."
  • Empty is the only warm one, and the only one with a primary action. Nothing is wrong — the user has arrived somewhere new, and the design's job is to get them started.
  • Filtered must never offer "Add product". That is the wrong action, and offering it implies the catalogue is empty when 214 items exist. It echoes the query back and offers the only useful move: undo the filter.
  • Failed must say nothing was lost. An error where a list should be reads as "my data is gone". One sentence fixes that, and it is the sentence most often missing.
  • Distinct at a glance and distinct to a screen reader. Three states that announce identically is the regression T-H names; the accessible text differs here by construction, not as a follow-up.
  • No illustrations. A drawing in an empty state is the clearest AI-slop tell in this category, and it delays the one control that matters.

Acceptance: for each of the 147 routes rendering a collection, five treatments are reachable and visibly different, and each announces differently. Track it as a checklist per module group, per E2.
